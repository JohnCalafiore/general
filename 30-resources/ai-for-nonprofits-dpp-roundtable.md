# AI for Nonprofits — DPP Roundtable notes (Aug 12, 2026)

Reference notes from the **Donor Participation Project** roundtable "AI and the Future of
Nonprofit Work," Wed Aug 12 2026, 10–11am MDT (Zoom), hosted by Louis Diez. Speakers:
**Gail Roberts, Paul Roach, Remy Reya**. John attended; captured in Granola.

Kept as a resource because it bears directly on DTD's own tooling decisions — the CRM
build, the grant-writing capacity question, and Andrew's stated goal of training Claude on
DTD's voice.

## The framing worth remembering

AI should **augment "head work"** (strategy, expertise) and **automate "hands work"** (data
entry, rote tasks). **"Heart work"** — relationships, empathy — stays human. That maps
cleanly onto a fundraising shop: research and drafting are fair game, the donor
relationship is not.

## Where AI is already winning

- **Grant writing** — major productivity gains synthesizing complex ideas and tailoring to
  individual funders.
- **Newsletters and routine copy** — largely AI-generated already; Gail put this ahead of
  the 5–10 year timeline others cited.
- **Prospect research** — tools like **iWave**, plus low-cost CRM analytics (**under
  $100/month**), remove the need for a dedicated prospect researcher.
- **Network-based relationship mapping** — **Happenstance** wraps an LLM around
  LinkedIn/Gmail to surface who in your network already knows a target contact.

## Change management

- **"Go slow to go far"** (Remy) — re-anchor in mission before rushing adoption.
- **Cross-functional working groups**, not top-down mandates (Gail).
- **Shadow AI use is common** — better surfaced than suppressed.
- Data-security fear is somewhat overhyped: paid models with correct settings are
  generally secure, and donor data can be de-identified.

## Preserving institutional wisdom

The risk flagged: AI smooths over the **"productive friction"** through which junior staff
build expertise. Remy's countermeasure is **"playbookification"** — use AI to interview
tenured staff through their actual decision-making, capturing judgment and taste rather
than process steps.

## Takeaways

- Ask "**is it good writing?**", not "is it AI-written?" — chasing AI detection is
  counterproductive.
- **Natural-language CRM interfaces** are most valuable for empowering leadership, not
  merely saving staff time.
- Tool integrations are improving fast — "if something doesn't connect today, check again
  in 6 months."

## Follow-ups suggested in the session

- Explore **Happenstance** (free tier available) and check whether it integrates with the
  CRM in use.
- Connect with Gail, Paul, and Remy on LinkedIn; Remy has shareable webinars on adjacent
  AI topics.
