# Why dinner — the positioning argument

John's own articulation of who DTD is for and why the format works, captured in a solo
working session on 2026-09-04 while preparing mission, vision and the pitch deck. This is
the **raw material for the vision/mission session with Jennifer Ashley on Sep 10** and for
the 5–6 slide deck that follows, and it is the clearest statement of the *argument* — as
distinct from [[30-resources/dinner-format-and-ground-rules|the format and ground rules]],
which describe the mechanics.

Nothing here is a finalized statement. No mission or vision was drafted in this session.

## Who the dinner is for

Three participant types, in priority order:

1. **Primary — the guy who wants more community, even if he isn't fully isolated.** Not a man
   in crisis. This is the significant framing choice: DTD is not built around rock bottom.
2. **Secondary — the "wit's end" guy**, "**sometimes pushed by a partner**."
3. **Secondary — the "built for this" guy** — someone already inclined toward this kind of
   room.

The underlying claim:

> **Men are communal by nature but pushed into isolation by remote work, friends with
> families**, and similar. DTD's low-barrier framing lets the **natural human instinct toward
> community do the heavy lifting**.

That is a different theory of change from most men's mental-health programming, which treats
connection as a skill to be taught. Here it is an instinct to be un-blocked.

## Why dinner, specifically

- **It sidesteps the stigma.** "*It's just dinner.*" No one has to accept a premise about
  themselves to come.
- **The physiology is real:** breaking bread triggers a **parasympathetic ("rest and digest")
  response**, which lowers defenses before anyone says anything.
- **The practical hook:** a good meal — BBQ, quality pizza — **that many guys don't often
  get**.
- **Thousands of years of precedent** as a human bonding ritual.
- The boundary, stated the same way every time: "**Not therapy, not counseling, not
  coaching.**"

## Brand language

- **"Third space" is preferred over "safe space."** A deliberate choice — "third space" is
  sociological and neutral (home, work, and the place that is neither), where "safe space"
  carries a therapeutic register DTD is trying to avoid.
- Tagline in use for social: "**Connecting Men in a Disconnected World**."

## What DTD gets right about men

- **Men do have emotional capacity — societal conditioning removes it, not the capacity
  itself.** The room doesn't teach men to feel; it removes the reasons not to.
- **The simplicity is the point:** a prompted conversation, **no feedback, no judgment**.
- The proof point worth telling: **an elderly man shared that his wife had died the week
  before, and the table held space.** Nobody fixed it. That is the product.
- Practitioners are validating it independently — **podcast invitations, partnership
  interest, "eureka" reactions** from mental-health professionals.
- **No RSVP, no commitment.** Men only need to know **there is a place, once a month**. In
  Idaho Springs, after one or two dinners, men said "**this has to continue.**"

## The acknowledged weakness

> **Testimonials are strong but hard data is a gap. Quantitative evidence of community impact
> is needed.**

This is the same gap the Monarch application addresses by adopting the **UCLA 3-Item
Loneliness Scale** and per-dinner roster tracking, and that the **Facilitator Feedback Form**
is being simplified to feed. Worth pairing when either is discussed: the story is strong, the
numbers are not yet, and both funders and corporates will ask.
