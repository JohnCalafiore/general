# The dinner format and ground rules

Canonical description of how a Dude Talk Dinner runs and the rules that govern it, taken
verbatim from the Monarch Mountain Community Grant application (Aug 2026) — the first place
in the record where the format and rules are written out in full rather than referred to.
Reuse this language in grant applications, the facilitator guide, the website, and
onboarding rather than re-deriving it.

## The format

> On a fixed night each month, [community] men gather for a **free dinner**. A facilitator
> **introduces a discussion topic, offers light framing, and then steps back and lets the
> Dudes talk**. It moves men past the sports-and-weather layer and into **relationships,
> work, health, grief, and fatherhood**.

## The ground rules

"Constant across every DTD community":

- **All men are welcome.**
- **What is shared here stays here.**
- **The dinner is substance and alcohol free.**
- **Speaking is not required, but respectful engagement is expected.**
- **No focus on politics or religion.**
- **No unsolicited advice.**
- **A recognition that this is not therapy, counseling, or coaching.** "It is dinner,
  conversation, and connection."

These same rules carry over to Discord as the moderation standard — facilitators moderate
their own community channels against "no politics/religion, no unsolicited advice,
confidentiality" (Leadership Circle, 2026-08-27).

## The operating model, in one paragraph

> **Our model is locally owned and centrally supported.** Local volunteers coordinate, host,
> and facilitate the dinners on the ground, because they know their own town. **Dude Central**
> carries the administrative weight: facilitator training, the reminder and engagement
> systems, the website and CRM, and the **Leadership Circle**, a monthly peer network where
> facilitators from every community support one another and solve problems together. We
> built it this way because **a community's survival depends almost entirely on how much work
> it takes to keep going. Take that work off local volunteers and the community lasts.**

## Why the design works — the reusable argument

> **A man will come to dinner who would be unlikely to book an appointment.** Everything about
> the design has a goal of eliminating the barriers to entry that keep men from attending
> mental health services. By offering free food from locally loved restaurants and meaningful
> connections without pressure or expectation, it feels very different from an intake
> questionnaire and talking to an insurance company middleman. **NIH research is clear that
> stigma and marginalization drive away exactly the people who most need support, so we
> removed as much of it as we could at the front door.**

Positioning against clinical services, for use where a funder may suspect duplication:

> Dude Talk Dinners is **upstream, non-clinical, free, and requires no appointment, no
> intake, and no admission that anything is wrong**. We reach the man who will never call
> existing agencies and we build the friendships that mean he may not have to.

## On measurement, and its limits

The stated balance to strike, which should govern any future survey design:

> We want to be able to strike a balance between where our dinner attendees can focus on
> connecting with each other vs filling out surveys and reporting data to our volunteer
> facilitators. **One of the weaknesses of our current mental health services is data
> collection for the sake of data collection**, and we aim to create an environment where
> **men don't feel like they are in a clinic doing another PHQ-9 depression measure**.

The instrument adopted for validated isolation measurement is the **UCLA 3-Item Loneliness
Scale**, listed in the Foundation for Social Connection's Social Connection Measurement Tools
Inventory.

And the honest statement of what the work is actually for:

> Beyond one year, the impact we are actually chasing is not measurable on a grant cycle. **We
> are trying to make it ordinary for a man in Buena Vista to have five people he could
> call.**... **It's the drug overdose or suicide attempt that doesn't happen**, in part,
> because of the sense of connection and belonging experienced by dinner attendees.
